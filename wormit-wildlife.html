Wildlife around Wormit

Wormit sits on the south shore of the Firth of Tay, in the shadow of the two Tay bridges. Mudflats, wooded burns and open farmland all sit within a short walk, so the mix of birds and mammals changes a lot with the tide and the time of year.

Wormit shore and the Tay Bridge views

Estuary foreshore, west of Wormit Bay towards the rail bridge

The tidal flats here are part of the Firth of Tay, a protected wildfowl and wader area. Come on a falling or rising tide and the edge of the mud fills with birds.

  • Redshank and curlewProbing the mud at low tide
  • Oystercatcher and dunlinOften in big, shifting flocks
  • Eider, goldeneye, red-breasted merganserDiving ducks out in the channel
  • Pink-footed geeseSkeins flying over at dawn and dusk
  • Common sealHauled out on sandbanks, more often seen further down the estuary
  • Sandwich and common ternFishing off the shore
  • Grey heron and cormorantStanding on the shoreline and bridge piers
  • GullsBlack-headed, herring and common gulls all year

Tip: aim for about two hours either side of high tide, when birds are pushed close to the sea wall.

Wormit Burn and Wormit Hill woodland

Wooded dens and paths behind the village

Mixed broadleaf woodland and the burn's gully give shelter for woodland birds and small mammals. It is quietest early in the morning.

  • Roe deerDawn and dusk along woodland edges
  • Great spotted woodpeckerListen for drumming in spring
  • Treecreeper, long-tailed tit, goldcrestOften in mixed flocks
  • Blackcap, chiffchaff, willow warblerSummer visitors singing from the canopy
  • Dipper or grey wagtailOn rocks in the burn, where water is fast and clean
  • Bluebells, wood anemone, wild garlicWoodland flowers from April
  • FungiFly agaric and other species after wet weather
  • Badger and foxMostly signs: tracks, latrines, diggings

Tay Road Bridge and Newport foreshore

The Fife landing of the road bridge and the shore towards Newport-on-Tay

Close to the bridge piers the current is strong, which draws fish and the animals that hunt them. Viewpoints along the path are good for scanning the water.

  • Bottlenose dolphinOccasionally seen in the Tay, more regularly nearer Broughty Ferry
  • Grey sealHeads popping up in the channel
  • Salmon and sea troutMigrating up the Tay, rarely seen directly
  • Peregrine falconHunting waders and pigeons around the bridge structures
  • Long-tailed duck and scoterRarer winter ducks further out
  • OtterOccasional on quiet shorelines at dawn; a lucky sighting

Tip: bring binoculars and scan slowly. Seal heads and dolphin fins are easy to miss on a choppy Tay.

Farmland and hedgerows toward Balmerino

Field edges, hedges and lanes west of Wormit

Arable fields and scrub corridors support birds that have declined elsewhere. They are best in late summer and through the winter stubble.

  • Yellowhammer and linnetOn hedge tops and fence posts
  • Buzzard and kestrelHovering or soaring above the fields
  • Fieldfare and redwingWinter thrushes stripping berries from hedges
  • Geese and whooper swansGrazing on stubble and winter crops
  • SkylarkSong-flight over open fields
  • Brown hareSpring is the time for boxing hares
  • ButterfliesPeacock, small tortoiseshell, meadow brown
  • Barn owlPossible at dusk along rough grass edges

Further afield: Tentsmuir and Lindores Loch

Short drives from Wormit; Tentsmuir is across the Tay at Tayport

Not on Wormit's doorstep but worth the trip. Tentsmuir National Nature Reserve has pine forest, dunes and a seal haul-out, while Lindores Loch is a small loch popular with wildfowl.

  • Common and grey seals at Tentsmuir PointHundreds can gather on the sandbars
  • Red squirrelIn the pine forest at Tentsmuir
  • Whooper swans and wigeonOn Lindores Loch
  • Dune plants and orchidsFlowering from late spring
  • WaxwingIrregular winter visitors; check local sightings
This guide shows species that are commonly or possibly recorded in this area, not guaranteed sightings. Check recent sightings on local birding groups, follow the Countryside Code, keep dogs under control and give seals and nesting birds plenty of space. Tide times and a local map are worth checking before you go.
